Intelligence Signal · Indonesia

Indonesia's sustainability reporting is entering a different phase.

The important change is not simply that new disclosure standards are coming. It is that sustainability information is moving closer to the language of financial decision-making.

September 2026 Regulation · Reporting · Markets Meridian ESG

For years, sustainability reporting could sit in a relatively separate part of the annual corporate conversation.

There was the financial report. There was the sustainability report. There were targets, programmes, policies and commitments.

That separation is becoming harder to maintain.

The signal

Indonesia is moving toward sustainability disclosure that is more closely connected to financial reporting and decision-making.

What has changed?

Indonesia's sustainability disclosure framework is moving toward the adoption of PSPK 1 and PSPK 2, which are aligned with the international IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards.

PSPK 1 covers general requirements for sustainability-related financial disclosures, while PSPK 2 focuses on climate-related disclosures. Both are scheduled to become effective on 1 January 2027. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

At the same time, OJK has been consulting on revisions to its sustainable-finance regulations for financial-sector entities, issuers and public companies. The proposed changes include stronger requirements around governance, risk management, strategy, metrics and targets, alongside alignment with PSPK 1 and PSPK 2. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Why this matters

The easy interpretation is that companies simply need to prepare for another reporting requirement.

That may be too narrow.

The more interesting question is what happens when sustainability information starts being treated less as a separate disclosure exercise and more as information relevant to how the business is understood.

That can affect who needs to be involved, what information gets collected, how risks are discussed and where sustainability issues enter management conversations.

The question behind the regulation

Will companies change how they manage sustainability — or simply become better at reporting it?

The transition may be more organisational than technical.

It is tempting to think of this as a reporting team's problem.

But the information required for meaningful sustainability-related financial disclosure rarely lives in one department.

It can involve finance, risk, operations, strategy, procurement, human resources, legal, investor relations and the people responsible for sustainability.

That creates a different challenge.

The question is no longer only whether the company can produce the disclosure. It is whether the company has the internal understanding and governance to produce information that actually reflects the business.

What to know

PSPK 1 and PSPK 2 were approved by IAI in July 2025 and are scheduled to take effect from 1 January 2027.

What is happening now

OJK has been developing revisions to its sustainable-finance framework, including proposed changes to reporting and disclosure requirements.

What to watch

The distance between sustainability reporting, financial reporting, risk management and corporate strategy.

Meridian Interpretation

The real transition may not be in the report.

It may be in the room where decisions are made.

A sustainability report can be produced by a highly capable reporting team without changing the way the wider organisation makes decisions.

But once sustainability-related financial information becomes more closely connected with governance, strategy, risk and investor decision-making, the boundary between “sustainability” and “the business” becomes much harder to defend.

That is where this transition becomes interesting.

The companies that treat the next phase as a disclosure exercise may focus on collecting information and satisfying requirements.

The companies that treat it as a management issue will have a different conversation: What do these risks mean for our business? Who owns them? What assumptions are we making? What needs to change?

The difference may not be visible in the report at first. It may be visible in the decisions that happen before the report is written.

What to watch next

01 · Governance

Who owns the information?

Watch whether sustainability information remains concentrated in specialist functions or becomes part of wider management and board processes.

02 · Strategy

Does disclosure change decisions?

The interesting evidence will be whether material sustainability risks and opportunities influence strategy, capital allocation and business priorities.

03 · Capability

Can the organisation explain the numbers?

Reliable disclosure requires more than data collection. It requires people across the organisation to understand what the information means.
